A breakthrough in magnetism could make AI chips use dramatically less energy.

Location: Brown University, United States
Researchers: A team led by Professor Lucas Caretta

Source: ScienceDaily – Read original news coverage | Brown University – Research announcement

1. What happened?

  • Researchers at Brown University have developed a new technique that could help make future computer chips consume less electricity. The researchers worked with a special material called bismuth ferrite (BiFeO3), which has both electrical and magnetic properties.
    The problem was that when this material was made extremely thin, it stopped functioning properly. Scientists call this the dead layer effect. The researchers developed a technique to overcome this problem, allowing the material to retain its electrical and magnetic properties even at extremely small thicknesses. This could eventually help engineers develop more energy-efficient computer chips, particularly for artificial intelligence.

3. How does the technology work?

The researchers used a special three-layer structure.

  1. Base layer: Provides support and helps maintain the material's atomic structure.
  2. Bismuth ferrite (BiFeO3): Maintains its electrical and magnetic properties even when extremely thin.
  3. Metallic layer: Helps control unwanted electric fields.

4. Why is this important?

Artificial intelligence requires enormous computing resources. As AI systems become more advanced, the demand for electricity also increases.

Earlier expectation: Thin materials cannot retain their electrical and magnetic properties. Researchers have developed a technique to overcome this problem.

AI data centres can consume 106 units of energy.

Research highlight

The research focuses on overcoming the dead layer problem in extremely thin multiferroic materials.
